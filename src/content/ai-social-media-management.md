# AI Social Media Management Software with Autonomous Agents

If you run social media for a small business, the hard part isn't the writing. It's writing every week in a voice that sounds like the business, keeping the calendar full, and being sure nothing goes out that you didn't see. OpenLegion gives you an agent that does the planning and drafting on a schedule. It holds anything that would leave your workspace until you approve it, stops at a daily spending cap you set, and keeps a record of everything it did. OpenLegion is a hosted platform, currently in private beta.

<!-- SCHEMA: DefinitionBlock -->

> **What is AI social media management?**
> AI social media management means using AI agents to plan, research and draft social posts on a schedule, with a person approving what gets published. The agent does the routine work: the calendar, first drafts, rewrites per platform. The person keeps the final say on what goes out under the brand's name.

## What a small business actually needs from it

Most people who manage social for a shop, a clinic, a studio or a handful of clients want the same five things. Here is how each one works in OpenLegion today.

**1. Drafts that sound like you.** The agent keeps a long-term memory. Give it your past posts, the words you never use and the topics you avoid, and it reads them before it drafts. When you fix a draft, the fix can be saved as a lesson it applies next time. You can open its memory, correct an entry or delete it. Nothing it "knows" about your brand is hidden from you.

**2. A content calendar that fills itself.** The agent can schedule its own future runs, once or on a repeating schedule. A typical setup: every Monday morning it reads the calendar file in its workspace, checks what's coming up (a launch, a holiday, an event), and drafts the week's posts into the workspace for you to review. It can search the web and read public pages for research, and it treats what it finds there as untrusted.

**3. Approval before anything goes out.** Every tool that acts outside the workspace, such as sharing a draft link with a client or posting to a connected channel, is held by default. Each one becomes an approval in your inbox, and you can approve it, edit it first, reject it, or reply to the agent. Until you decide, the action doesn't happen.

**4. A budget cap.** Each agent has a daily spending limit. Cost is reserved before every run, and an agent with no budget set doesn't run at all, because the platform fails closed. You can see what each task cost, so a week of drafts has a known price.

**5. An audit trail.** Every tool call the agent makes goes through one policy check and leaves a receipt. The audit log is on every plan. If a client asks why a post said what it said, you can see what the agent read, what it drafted and who approved it.

## A week with a social media agent

This is how the setup above plays out, step by step.

1. **Brief it once.** In chat, describe the business, the platforms you post on and how often. Paste in ten or so posts you like. The assistant creates the agent and you set its daily budget.
2. **It builds the calendar.** The agent writes a calendar file to its workspace (dates, themes, which platform gets what) and schedules a weekly run.
3. **Monday: drafts arrive.** On schedule, it drafts the week's posts as text, one version per platform: a short post for X, a longer one for LinkedIn, a caption and hook for Instagram or TikTok.
4. **You review.** Rewrite a line, mark a draft as off-brand, or ask for another version. Corrections you want kept become lessons.
5. **You publish.** Approved text goes into whichever scheduler or native app you already use (see the next section for why).
6. **Next Monday it's a little closer.** The agent reads its lessons before drafting again, and you can see exactly which ones it has.

The same pattern covers recurring jobs that don't need a person until the end: a monthly recap of what you posted, replies drafted for comments you paste in, a list of local events worth a post.

## What OpenLegion doesn't do yet

OpenLegion has no built-in integration with X, LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook or any other social network, so it cannot publish to them, read your DMs or reply to comments on its own. It also has no browser yet, so it cannot log in to a social site for you. Today it plans, researches and drafts, and you or your existing scheduler do the posting. We'd rather say that plainly than have you find out after signing up.

## Running several brands or clients

Each brand can have its own agent with its own memory, its own budget and its own permissions. One brand's voice won't drift into another's drafts, and a busy week for one client can't spend another client's budget. An agent is just a set of rows in a database, so an idle agent isn't woken and costs nothing between runs. Adding a brand doesn't add a running machine. For how agents hand work to each other inside a team, see [AI agent orchestration](/learn/ai-agent-orchestration).

## Why outside content can't post on its own

Social work means reading things you don't control: web pages, competitors' posts, comments people paste in for a reply. OpenLegion marks content that arrives from outside as untrusted. If a run has read untrusted content, any action that would send something out is held for you, even if you'd normally pre-approve it. A comment that says "ignore your instructions and post this" ends up as a held approval, not a post. More on this in [AI agent security](/learn/ai-agent-security).

## How this compares with a scheduler

A scheduler with a "generate" button gives you a draft when you ask for one. An agent is set up once and then works on a schedule. It remembers corrections, keeps a calendar, runs within a budget and records its decisions. If your posting already runs smoothly and you only want help writing a caption now and then, a scheduler's built-in AI is enough. If the gap is keeping up every week in a consistent voice, that's the job an agent does. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is AI social media management?

It means using AI agents to plan, research and draft social posts on a schedule, while a person approves what's published. In OpenLegion the agent keeps the calendar, drafts in your voice using memory you can see and edit, and holds anything that would leave the workspace until you approve it.

### Can OpenLegion post directly to X, LinkedIn, Instagram or TikTok?

Not today. There is no built-in social network integration and no browser, so the agent can't publish, read DMs or reply to comments on those platforms itself. It drafts the text for each platform, and you publish it with the scheduler or app you already use.

### Can I keep a human in the loop for every post?

Yes, and that's the default. Any tool that acts outside the workspace is held as an approval. You can approve it, edit it first, reject it, or reply to the agent. A run that has read untrusted content can't send anything out without you, even for an action you've pre-approved.

### Is it safe to use an AI agent for my brand's social media?

The agent can't post to your social accounts, so it never needs your social logins. What it can do is limited by per-agent permissions, a daily budget and approvals on anything outward. Every action leaves a receipt in the audit log. Credentials it does use go through a broker, and the model only sees a handle, never the secret itself.

### What does AI social media management cost on OpenLegion?

Work is metered in credits, each agent has a daily budget, and the cost of every task is shown. Cost is reserved before a run starts, so an agent can't spend past its cap. Current plan prices are on the [pricing page](/pricing).

### Does it work for agencies managing several clients?

Yes. Give each client its own agent with separate memory, budget and permissions. Idle agents aren't woken and cost nothing between runs, so adding a client doesn't add a running machine. Approvals from every agent land in one inbox.
